Emergency Frozen Pipe Water Damage in Port Washington, NY

Ice blocks a pipe in winter, and the real damage often starts when it thaws and the split begins to leak.

☎ Call (516) 845-9582

Frozen pipe water damage happens when ice blocks a line, the pressure builds behind it, and the pipe splits or a joint fails. The water often appears only after the thaw. If you suspect a freeze, call the 24/7 line, open the faucet the pipe feeds, and shut the main if you see water. Do not use flame to thaw the line.

On Long Island's North Shore, a hard freeze is a normal part of winter. Pipes in unheated or poorly insulated spaces are the ones at risk. The visit starts with finding where the ice is, then where the split is, and then how far the water spread.

Why North Shore winters put pipes at risk

Water expands as it freezes, and it is the pressure between the ice plug and the closed faucet that cracks the pipe. A freeze does not always break a line. Often the pipe stays intact until the ice melts and flow resumes, and then the weakened spot gives way.

That is why the damage is often found on a mild day after the cold snap. The leak you notice at noon may have started at dawn.

A pipe is frozen or thawing right now

If you suspect ice in a line, move in this order.

Open the faucet

Leave the tap on so melting water has somewhere to go and pressure does not build.

Shut the main if water appears

If you see a wet wall, drip or stain, close the main shutoff right away.

Warm the area gently

Raise the room temperature and use a hair dryer on low aimed at the pipe, starting near the faucet and working back.

Open cabinet doors

Let warm air reach pipes on exterior walls under sinks.

Call for help

If the pipe is behind a wall or you cannot see it, stop. Call the 24/7 line and a licensed plumber.

What not to do with a frozen pipe

Safety first

Never use a torch, open flame, kerosene heater or propane device on a frozen pipe. Fire and carbon monoxide are the risks, and the heat can crack the pipe and ignite what is behind the wall. Do not touch outlets or wiring near wet areas. If the pipe is inside a wall, do not cut it open yourself.

What a split pipe does after the thaw

A split in a supply line releases full-pressure water once flow returns. The water soaks insulation first, then the back of the drywall, then the framing and floor below. A small crack can leak for hours before it shows.

For fast action, the steps on the burst pipe page apply once the pipe has failed. A split in a basement ceiling or crawl space may also involve the crawl space page. Either way, the pipe is repaired by a plumber while restoration removes water and dries the structure.

Repair options after a freeze

Fixing the pipe and fixing the cause are two jobs.

Replace the split section

A plumber cuts out the damaged length and splices in new pipe. It is quick but will not help the next cold spot.

Insulate the line

Foam sleeves or wrap protect exposed pipes at low cost, but they do not heat anything.

Reroute or relocate

Moving a line off an exterior wall solves the problem for good but means opening walls.

Seal air leaks

Caulking and foam around rim joists and penetrations cut cold drafts on pipes.

Drying the wall cavity and floor

After the leak is stopped, water is removed and the cavity is opened if readings are high. Wet insulation and wet studs show up on moisture readings and thermal images. What remains is dried with air movers and low-grain refrigerant units, as the structural drying page explains.

Wet insulation does not dry well and is usually replaced. Drywall that stayed wet is cut back to dry material and patched. Quick action keeps this small.

Cost factors for frozen pipe damage

The total follows what the water touched, not the length of the cold snap.

  • Water category: clean supply water costs the least, and the price rises if it crossed dirty materials.
  • Affected area: one wall is a small job and a whole floor is a larger one.
  • Materials: insulation, drywall and flooring are handled differently.
  • Equipment and drying days: machines stay until the wall reads dry.
  • Rebuild scope: drywall, paint and trim after drying. A call to dispatch is followed by a plain explanation of scope and price ahead of any work.

Insurance and freezing pipes

Sudden water damage from a frozen pipe that bursts is often covered, but policies sometimes require that the home was kept heated. Neglect is often excluded. Photograph everything, save receipts for emergency work, and read your policy.

If your home sits empty in winter, ask a neighbor to check it. Keep the heat on and open cabinet doors on exterior walls.

Frequently asked questions

Do you handle frozen pipe emergencies at night or on weekends in Port Washington?

Yes. Cold snaps tend to bring overnight and weekend calls, and dispatch picks up at any hour. A same-day visit is possible when a crew is free. Open the tap and close the main if you see water.

Can I thaw a frozen pipe myself?

If the pipe is exposed, gentle warm air from a hair dryer can work. If it is inside a wall or already leaking, stop and call.

How do I know if a frozen pipe has burst?

Look for a wet wall, drip, stain or sound of water once the pipe thaws. A water meter that keeps spinning with every tap closed points to a leak too.

Why did my pipe leak only after it thawed?

The ice was plugging the crack. Once it melted, water flowed through the split.

Will insurance cover frozen pipe damage?

Often if it was sudden and accidental, but conditions apply. Ask your insurer.

Do you repair the pipe itself?

A licensed plumber does the pipe repair. Restoration handles the water removal and drying.
