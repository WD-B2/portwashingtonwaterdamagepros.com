Kitchen Water Damage in Port Washington, NY

Cabinets, toe-kicks, countertops and the subfloor beneath them, dried in place where that works and opened up where it does not.

☎ Call (516) 845-9582

Kitchen water damage in Port Washington usually starts out of sight. A supply line splits under the sink, a pipe in the wall behind the range lets go, and the water runs into the cabinet base, the toe-kick and the subfloor long before anyone sees a puddle. The job is to locate each wet layer, dry what can stay and take out what cannot.

A kitchen is hard to dry because it packs many materials into a small room. Solid wood, plywood, particleboard, laminate and tile each absorb and release water on their own schedule. This page covers cabinets, countertops, toe-kicks and the floor system under them. If a dishwasher, refrigerator line or similar machine caused the leak, the appliance page covers that side of the problem.

A pipe bursts under the sink

The most common call for this room is a failed pipe or fitting inside the sink base. Water pours onto the cabinet floor, finds the gap around the drain and supply openings, and drops to the subfloor. Because the cabinet door stays shut, a leak can run overnight or while the household is at work.

Close the angle stop under the sink or the main, clear the cabinet and take pictures before wiping. Towels help with the surface, but they do nothing for the water that has already moved under the cabinet box. For the pipe failure itself, the burst pipe page explains how breaks are located and repaired.

What a wet kitchen looks like

These signs often show up days after a slow or hidden leak starts.

  • The toe-kick board at the base of the cabinets feels soft, swollen or has begun to separate.
  • The inside floor of the sink cabinet is stained, bubbled or crumbles when pressed.
  • Laminate countertop edges near the sink have lifted or the seam has opened.
  • A musty smell comes from the cabinets even when the sink is dry.
  • Floor tile or vinyl near the cabinets has loosened, cupped or sounds hollow.
  • The water meter keeps moving when every fixture in the house is off.

How the damage is found and measured

The sequence matters because a dry-looking surface can sit on a wet core.

Map the moisture

Meters and a thermal camera follow the water behind the toe-kick, under the dishwasher bay and along the cabinet backs.

Classify the water

A clean supply line is category 1. Drain water or anything that sat for days is category 2 or 3, which changes what may stay.

Remove what cannot be saved

Swollen particleboard, soaked insulation and wet toe-kick material come out first so they stop holding water against the framing.

Dry the rest

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run on the cabinet interiors and floor, sometimes with drying mats or small injection ports for closed cavities.

Verify with readings

Moisture readings are taken again against unaffected material in the same room until the wet areas match it.

Dry in place or tear out

Honest trade-offs differ by material, and the decision is made with a meter, not by looks alone.

MaterialOften dried in placeUsually removed
Solid wood cabinet boxesYes, if clean water and caught earlyWarped or delaminated parts
Plywood boxesOften, with open dryingSwollen edges that do not recover
Particleboard or MDFRarely, it swells and weakensMost wet pieces
Laminate countertopSurface only, if the seam is sealedLifted or bubbled sections
Subfloor under cabinetsYes, with floor-level dryingSoft or delaminated panels

Why particleboard is the weak link

Many kitchen cabinets are built from particleboard with a thin laminate skin. Once water gets into the core, the board swells and loses its holding strength, and it rarely shrinks back to a flat, sound shape. Drying can stop further damage, but the swollen piece may still need to be replaced.

Solid wood and good plywood behave better. They can often be dried while the cabinets stay mounted, which saves the cost and disruption of pulling the whole run. The catch is that drying under a fixed cabinet needs openings, so a few small holes or a removed toe-kick are normal.

What drives the cost

No fair figure exists until someone has measured the room. These factors set the scope:

  • The water category, since category 2 or 3 water means more removal and cleaning.
  • How far the water traveled beyond the sink base into adjacent cabinets and rooms.
  • The cabinet and countertop materials, and whether they can be dried or must be replaced.
  • The number of drying days and how much equipment the room needs.
  • How much rebuild work follows, from a new toe-kick to a full cabinet run.

Insurance and your kitchen

A burst supply line under the sink is the kind of sudden event a homeowners policy often covers. Slow leaks and long-term neglect often are not, so the cause matters. Put the specifics to your insurer.

Photograph the cabinets, floor and any failed part before cleanup, and keep receipts for emergency repairs. State insurance regulators advise waiting for the insurer's inspection before permanent repairs.

Kitchens around Port Washington

North Shore winters bring freezing weather, which can stress supply lines to a kitchen on an exterior wall. A supply line in an exterior wall can freeze and split, then leak behind the cabinets once it thaws. See the frozen pipe page if that sounds like your case.

Frequently asked questions

Can kitchen cabinets be saved after a leak?

Often yes if the boxes are solid wood or plywood and the water was clean. Particleboard that has swollen usually has to be replaced.

Do you have to remove the countertop?

Not always. Stone and sealed laminate can often stay if water did not get under them, but a lifted seam or a soaked substrate may force removal.

How is the subfloor under the cabinets dried?

Through the toe-kick opening or small ports, using air movers and dehumidifiers, then confirmed with moisture readings.

Is a leak under the sink an emergency?

Shut off the water first. If water is still reaching the floor or the cabinet is soaked, call the 24/7 line the same day.

Will insurance cover the damage?

Sudden and accidental leaks are often covered while slow leaks often are not. Your policy wording settles the question.

What about leaks from a dishwasher or fridge line?

Those are covered on the appliance leak page, including how the source is stopped.
