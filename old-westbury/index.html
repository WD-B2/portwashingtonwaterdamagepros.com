Old Westbury is a village of big lots and bigger houses, and water damage restoration here is shaped by that scale. This page starts with the housing itself, because the way Old Westbury was built explains most of the water problems we are asked about: finished lower levels, wood floors laid over large rooms, and plumbing that stretches a long way from the main.

Estate-sized houses and what that does to a water loss

After the Civil War, wealthy families built horse, fox-hunting and polo estates across this part of Long Island. Old Westbury Gardens, the former John Shaffer Phipps estate and a museum home since 1959, still shows the scale. Large houses on large parcels mean long runs of supply and drain pipe, several water heaters and more bathrooms per floor than a typical home.

The practical result is that a failure far from the main valve can run for a long time before it reaches a visible wall. If the house has been shut for a stretch, a slow leak may go unseen for weeks, so check unused wings and lower levels first.

Before we arrive, if water is still spreading

In a large house the first ten minutes matter mostly for stopping flow. Here is the order that works.

Find the right valve

Big houses often have zone or fixture valves besides the main. Close the nearest one, and if water keeps coming, close the main.

Treat the panel with caution

Breakers for wet zones are worth flipping only from dry footing at a dry panel. Any doubt, stay back and ring the utility.

Rescue the high-value items

Lift rugs, art, instruments and electronics out of the wet zone. Place furniture legs on foil or plastic blocks.

Keep everyone clear

Water that backed up from a drain, toilet or outside is not safe to wade through. Keep children, staff and pets in dry rooms.

Document the loss

Photograph and video each room before moving things, then reach dispatch on the 24/7 line.

The Long Island Expressway and the 1948 secession

The Long Island Expressway was cut through Old Westbury in the late 1950s and early 1960s, and a decade earlier, in 1948, voters let 375 acres on the western edge secede. That land became the Roslyn Country Club area, which now sits in Roslyn Heights. If your house is near that line, the building and utility details can differ from the house next door.

Old Westbury itself was incorporated in May 1924, so the village has a long record of its own rules. Ask the village building department before starting repairs that need a permit.

Basements first, wood floors close behind

Finished lower levels in large houses often hold media rooms, wine storage, gyms and guest suites. When water arrives, whether from a sump failure, a supply line or a heavy storm, basement water damage cleanup starts with extraction units and ends with LGR dehumidifiers running until readings return to normal.

Upstairs and on the main level, hardwood is the other common casualty. Boards cup when moisture rises from below, and a slow leak under a kitchen or bath can lift a whole room. Hardwood floor water damage repair depends on catching it early, since quick drying sometimes saves boards that would otherwise be replaced.

How a drying job runs in a large house

Size changes the logistics but not the order of work.

Find and stop the water

We locate the source, confirm the valve is closed and check for live electrical hazards.

Extract standing water

Extractors lift the standing water first, then crews go back along the baseboards for what pooled at the edges.

Map the moisture

Meter readings and infrared images show where the water went inside walls and below floors, which in a big house can be a long way from the leak.

Set drying equipment

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers are placed room by room, with more units for larger volumes.

Monitor until dry

Readings are checked daily, equipment is moved, and drying ends only when materials reach normal levels.

Scope the repairs

Before any rebuild begins, we lay out the scope and what it will cost.

Two towns, one village hall

Check who issues permits

Because the village sits in two towns, do not assume the Town of North Hempstead or the Town of Oyster Bay handles your permit. Ask the village building department. We do not guess which office covers your street, and neither should a contractor you hire.

Cost factors and insurance for estate-scale losses

Price comes down to water category, affected area, materials, drying days with equipment, and rebuild scope. Large rooms and tall ceilings raise the air volume to be dried, which raises equipment counts. You hear the scope and price before work starts.

On insurance, the split usually falls between a sudden failure and a slow one, and between plumbing water and flood or back-up water, which are often their own coverage lines. Read your policy and talk to your insurer. Confirm your coverage early, then call for water extraction so standing water comes out before it spreads. Frequently asked questions

Do you cover every ZIP code in Old Westbury?

Old Westbury uses one ZIP, 11568, and we cover it across both the North Hempstead and Oyster Bay portions of the village. That includes the area around Old Westbury Gardens, the NYIT and SUNY Old Westbury campuses and the streets on either side of the Long Island Expressway.

Is emergency water removal available in Old Westbury?

Yes. Emergency dispatch is available 24 hours a day, every day of the year, from our Port Washington base. Tell the dispatcher which floor the water is on.

Why do large houses suffer bigger losses from small leaks?

Longer pipe runs and more fixtures give a leak more places to hide. Water also has farther to travel inside walls and floors, so damage can be wide before it is visible.

Can hardwood floors be saved after a leak?

Often, if drying starts quickly. Boards that have cupped but not buckled can sometimes flatten as moisture comes down. Floors left wet for long periods usually need partial or full replacement.

Which building department handles permits in Old Westbury?

Old Westbury has its own village building department. Ask them first, since the village spans two towns and you should not assume which town office applies.

Do you handle campus or commercial buildings?

Yes, scoped separately from a home. Larger buildings need a plan for access, occupants and equipment placement, so describe the space when you call.
