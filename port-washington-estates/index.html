Water Damage Restoration in Port Washington Estates, NY

What the National Weather Service log says about this area, and what to do when water gets in.

☎ Call (516) 845-9582

Port Washington Estates is a neighborhood the Town of North Hempstead's civic association list files under Port Washington, where it appears as the Port Washington Estates Association. A neighborhood guide describes it as single-family homes just south of Main Street and off Mackey Avenue. Sources do not state its incorporation status, so confirm what governs your street.

Water damage restoration here means removing water, drying the structure and documenting the loss. One public record names this neighborhood, and it deserves careful reading, so that is where we start.

What the National Weather Service log says

The New York office of the National Weather Service publishes a coastal flood impact log. For 3/14/2017 and 12/30/2019 it reads: "Minor flooding on Shore Road, Port Washington Estates and Sunset Park on Main Street. No property damage reported."

That is a coastal flood impact record for those two dates, not a statement about every home. It reports no property damage, and it says nothing about your basement, your street or your next storm.

Checking your own address

Neither the log nor this page tells you your FEMA status. Use these steps instead.

  • Search FEMA's Flood Map Service Center for your street.
  • Treat any mapped zone as a label, not a forecast for your house.
  • Ask your insurer whether your policy covers flood or only sudden indoor water.
  • Ask about a sewer or water back-up endorsement.
  • Keep your policy documents where you can reach them after a loss.

Safety first

Safety first

Water meeting electricity is the first danger. Do not wade through it toward outlets, extension cords or the panel, and do not touch breakers or plug in equipment while you stand wet. A supply line that is still running can be closed at its valve, but only if the valve is dry and within easy reach.

Call 911 or the utility at once for any electrical or gas hazard. Flood or sewage water should go to a professional crew, never a household fan or vacuum.

Water that starts inside the house

Most calls for a home like yours are not about a storm. Think supply lines, water heaters, appliances, toilets, leaking roofs or windows, and sewer backups. This is a general service explanation, and we make no claim about how common any of these are in this neighborhood.

When a drain line or floor drain pushed the water up, sewage backup cleanup applies. If it came from a failed heater tank, see water heater leak cleanup.

Pulling the claim together

Photograph the damage before cleanup, then build a room-by-room inventory and save receipts. According to the state's DFS, ordinary homeowners coverage skips flood, which usually needs its own NFIP or private policy, and back-up coverage may be added by endorsement. Read your policy first, and ask your insurer what applies.

For storm-driven water, see storm water damage cleanup.

Mold and permits

Mold needs moisture, so drying is the first defense. State law calls for licensed mold assessors and remediators, kept as separate people on any one property. Ask any company about its licence.

We cannot say who issues permits for your street. Confirm with the building department that covers it before any rebuild. The wider picture sits on the Port Washington water damage restoration page. Before you book, ask the company about its licensing and insurance.

Frequently asked questions

Does the National Weather Service log mean my house floods?

No. It is a coastal flood impact record for 3/14/2017 and 12/30/2019 that reports minor flooding and no property damage reported. It is not a statement about every home.

Is Port Washington Estates in a village?

The sources do not state its incorporation status. The Town's civic list files it under Port Washington, so check which building department handles your block.

How do I find my flood zone?

Use FEMA's Flood Map Service Center for your own address. Mapped zones describe areas, and they say little about one house in one storm.

Is flood damage covered by homeowners insurance?

Often not. The state's DFS says typical policies exclude flood, so read yours and ask your insurer.

When is drying finished?

When logged moisture readings match dry reference points for each material, not when a room feels dry to the touch.
