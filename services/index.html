Water Damage Services in Port Washington, NY

Every service we handle, sorted by where the water came from.

☎ Call (516) 845-9582

Water damage looks different depending on its source, and the right response depends on it too. A supply line burst needs a different plan than a rising storm surge or a failed washer hose. The groups below sort each page by cause so you can go straight to the one that matches what you are seeing. Each page explains the damage, the equipment used, the cost factors and what to do in the first hours.

Pipes and plumbing

Failed lines inside the home are among the most common reasons people call. A burst pipe floods quickly, while a frozen pipe often shows up after a cold snap, when the thaw reveals a split. A hidden water heater leak or a slab leak can wet a floor for a long time before anyone notices. Pages in this group also explain which valve to close and when a plumber needs to come first.

Appliances and HVAC

Machines that use water eventually leak. Washers, dishwashers and refrigerator lines are covered under appliance leak damage, and a clogged condensate line is the usual story behind an AC leak. Because so much goes wrong in this room, kitchen water damage has its own page for cabinets, floors and toe-kicks. These losses are usually Category 1 or 2 water when caught early.

Not sure which page fits? Call the 24/7 line and describe the water.

Weather and building envelope

Rain and groundwater get in through the roof, walls, windows and foundation. A wet ceiling often traces back to roof or upstairs plumbing, and storm damage covers wind-driven rain and debris. Larger events are handled as flood damage, and lower levels have their own guidance for a basement or a crawl space.

Water removal and drying

These pages describe the work itself rather than the source. Water extraction removes bulk water with pumps and extraction units, and structural drying uses air movers and LGR dehumidifiers to finish the job in walls and framing. When the water came from a drain or sewer line, see sewage backup cleanup, which has extra safety steps. Most losses use more than one of these, in order: remove the water, then dry what remains.

Floors and walls

Finished surfaces react differently to water. Hardwood floors cup and buckle, carpet depends on the water category and how quickly the pad is lifted, and wall and drywall damage often hides wet insulation behind the paint. Every page below covers one cause or one affected material, so start with the one closest to what you see.

Commercial properties

Shops, offices and multi-unit buildings add business continuity, tenants and shared systems to the problem. The page for commercial water damage restoration explains how a loss in a building like that is scoped and staged. Call early, because downtime is the main cost a business owner feels.

Mold remediation, our secondary service

When water sat long enough for growth to start, mold remediation covers the independent assessment, containment and clearance process. It comes after the water source is fixed and the structure is dry.

How to choose between pages

Start with the source of the water, not the room. If a supply line or drain failed, begin with the pipes or removal groups. If rain or groundwater came in, begin with weather. Staining with no obvious source is a normal reason to call, since dispatch can help you work backward from what you see.

Whatever page you land on, the first steps are the same: make the area safe, stop the water if you can and photograph the damage. For background, the about page tells how jobs run, the service area page lists the towns reached, and the home page has the overview.
